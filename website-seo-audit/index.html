Website SEO audit

A website SEO audit that shows what to fix, and why

Scoutly’s website SEO audit crawls the pages search engines can reach, checks the technical and on-page signals that affect how those pages are discovered and understood, and turns the results into a prioritized list of findings. Each finding shows the affected URLs, the evidence behind it, a plain-English explanation and a suggested fix.

Live

The audit runs on a real crawl: the page you enter plus internal pages up to three clicks deep, 25 pages in total, skipping anything robots.txt disallows, with up to 120 links checked. It doesn’t include backlink or keyword data. Public accounts aren’t open yet, so audits run in the preview workspace.

What the audit checks

Six areas of technical and on-page SEO

An audit first looks at your site the way a crawler does: can pages be found, fetched and indexed? Then it checks whether each page clearly communicates what it’s about.

Crawlability and indexability

Robots.txt rules, meta robots and X-Robots-Tag directives, XML sitemap availability, canonical tags and HTTP status codes. Together they decide whether a page can be crawled and whether it’s eligible to be indexed.

On-page SEO

Page titles, meta descriptions, heading structure, image alt attributes, and duplicate or missing metadata across pages. These help search engines and visitors understand each page’s topic.

Broken links and redirects

Links returning 4xx or 5xx errors, redirect chains with several hops, redirect loops, and internal links that point to a redirect instead of the final URL. See the broken link checker for detail.

Internal linking

Orphaned pages with no internal links pointing to them, important pages buried deep in the site, and pages with few internal connections. Internal links help crawlers find pages and show how your content fits together.

Performance and Core Web Vitals

Largest Contentful Paint, Interaction to Next Paint and Cumulative Layout Shift, from the PageSpeed Insights API, using Chrome field data where it exists for your URL. Google’s shared keyless quota often runs out, so reliable results need your own free PageSpeed API key.

Structured data

JSON-LD markup, checked for syntax errors and missing properties on common schema.org types. Microdata and RDFa are detected but not validated. Valid markup can make a page eligible for some rich results, but it doesn’t guarantee them.

Prioritized findings

Critical problems first, routine improvements after

A large site can produce hundreds of findings. Scoutly sorts each one into three levels, so you start with whatever blocks discovery instead of whatever is merely untidy.

Critical issues

Pages that may not be crawled or indexed

For example, an important page blocked in robots.txt, a stray noindex tag, or a canonical tag pointing to the wrong URL.

Improvements

Problems that weaken navigation or clarity

Broken internal links, redirect chains, duplicate page titles, missing meta descriptions and weak internal connections.

Optimization opportunities

Changes that can make good pages better

Clearer headings, stronger internal links to important pages, expanded topic coverage and better-structured answers.

Every finding answers four questions

  • What was detected: the exact issue and the URLs it affects.
  • Why it matters: how it can affect crawling, indexing or visitors.
  • How to verify it: a way to confirm the problem yourself, so you don’t have to take a score on trust.
  • What to do next: a suggested fix, written for whoever will make the change.

Severity reflects how directly an issue affects crawling, indexing and usability. It isn’t a prediction of ranking changes, because rankings depend on many factors no audit can measure.

How an audit runs

From a URL to a fix list in four steps

  1. Enter a URL

    Start with your homepage or any other page. The crawl begins there and follows links within the same site.

  2. Crawl

    Scoutly reads robots.txt and your XML sitemap, then follows internal links the way a search engine crawler does: up to 25 pages, three clicks deep, skipping anything robots.txt disallows.

  3. Analyze

    Each fetched page is checked against the audit rules and up to 120 links are tested. Core Web Vitals are added from PageSpeed Insights when its quota allows.

  4. Review and compare

    Findings are grouped by severity. Re-run the audit later to see what’s fixed, what’s new and what’s still open.

When to run an audit

Good moments to audit your website

Before and after a launch

Catch blocked pages, missing titles and broken navigation before visitors and crawlers do.

During a migration or redesign

Domain moves, URL changes and new templates are where redirect chains and lost pages usually appear.

After big content changes

Removing, merging or reorganizing pages can leave broken links and orphaned content behind.

On a regular schedule

Sites that change often benefit from repeat audits, so new issues are caught while they’re still small.

Know the limits

What an SEO audit can and can’t tell you

An audit is a diagnostic tool, not a ranking forecast. It can show you that a page is blocked, slow or missing a title. It can’t tell you exactly how a search engine weighs those issues against your content, your competitors or what searchers want.

  • Pages that aren’t linked from anywhere and aren’t in your sitemap may not be discovered by any crawler.
  • Each audit covers up to 25 pages and 120 link checks, so on a large site it’s a sample, not a full crawl.
  • Scoutly doesn’t run JavaScript. Content that only appears after scripts run is flagged, not checked.
  • Sites that block automated requests or rate-limit crawlers can produce incomplete results.
  • Real-world Core Web Vitals data only exists for URLs with enough Chrome traffic.

FAQ

Website SEO audit questions

What’s the difference between a technical SEO audit and an on-page audit?

A technical audit looks at whether search engines can crawl, render and index your pages: status codes, robots directives, canonical tags, sitemaps, redirects and performance. An on-page audit looks at what each page communicates: titles, descriptions, headings, content structure and image alt text. Scoutly’s audit covers both in a single crawl.

Will the audit change anything on my website?

No. An audit only reads publicly accessible pages, the same way a browser or search engine crawler does. You or your team make the fixes in your CMS or codebase.

Does Scoutly respect robots.txt?

Yes. Scoutly’s crawler reads robots.txt and skips any page disallowed for it or for all crawlers. The audit also reports which pages are blocked for search engine crawlers, because that’s a common cause of indexing problems.

How many pages can be audited?

Each audit checks the page you enter plus internal pages found by following links up to three clicks deep, 25 pages in total, and tests up to 120 links. On a large site that’s a sample, so start from a section’s main page to focus the crawl there.

Will fixing every issue improve my rankings?

Not necessarily. Fixing technical problems removes obstacles to crawling and indexing, which matters. But rankings also depend on content quality and relevance, competition, and how search engines evaluate your site. No tool can guarantee specific rankings.
