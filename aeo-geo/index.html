AEO and GEO

AEO and GEO: get your content ready for answer engines and AI search

Answer engine optimization (AEO) and generative engine optimization (GEO) are about making useful content easy for search systems to find, understand and reference when they answer questions. They build on SEO rather than replacing it. Scoutly’s readiness checks show where your pages are already clear, and where they could be easier to understand.

Live

Basic AEO and GEO readiness checks now run in every website SEO audit. They check your pages, not AI answers: tracking mentions and citations isn’t built yet. Nothing on this page implies guaranteed placement in AI-generated answers.

Definitions

What AEO and GEO mean

Answer engine optimization (AEO)

Structuring useful content so that questions are answered clearly, pages are easy to navigate, and relevant facts can be understood by search systems, including featured snippets, voice assistants and AI answers.

Generative engine optimization (GEO)

Improving content clarity, topical depth, source quality and entity information, to support discoverability in search experiences that generate answers instead of only listing links.

How they relate to SEO

Answer engines and generative search still depend on finding and understanding pages. Crawlability, indexing, clear structure and trustworthy content remain the foundation of all three.

Readiness checklist

What makes content easier to understand and cite

Questions answered directly

Lead with a short, direct answer, then add detail. Readers and systems can both find the answer without guessing.

Clear structure

Descriptive headings, short sections, and lists or tables where they help. Each section should make sense on its own.

Crawlable and indexable pages

Many AI search features rely on search indexes. Check that important pages aren’t blocked and that key content is in the HTML.

Clear entity information

Use a consistent brand name, a helpful About page, and organization details, so it’s clear who is behind the content.

Evidence and sources

Cite data and sources, show who wrote or reviewed the content, and keep dates accurate when information changes.

Accurate structured data

Schema.org markup that matches the visible content helps machines interpret a page. It supports understanding but doesn’t guarantee placement.

AI crawlers and robots.txt

Decide which AI crawlers can access your content

Several AI companies publish the user agents their crawlers use and say they respect robots.txt. Some separate crawling for model training from crawling for search features, so you can allow one and not the other.

Google, for example, provides the Google-Extended token to control whether content is used for its Gemini models, and says it doesn’t affect inclusion or ranking in Google Search.

Whether to allow AI crawlers is a business decision with trade-offs. Check each provider’s current documentation before changing your rules, because crawler names and behavior change.

Common myths

What AEO and GEO aren’t

A guaranteed citation

No technique guarantees that an AI system will mention or cite you. Anyone promising that is overselling.

A replacement for SEO

Answer and generative search still rely on discovering and understanding pages. Technical SEO and helpful content still come first.

A special file or tag

No single file or markup type makes a page appear in AI answers. Proposals such as llms.txt exist, but they aren’t an established standard and support varies.

FAQ

AEO and GEO questions

What’s the difference between AEO and GEO?

AEO focuses on answering questions clearly so answer-oriented features can use your content. GEO focuses more broadly on how generative search systems understand and reference content: clarity, depth, sources and entity information. In practice they overlap heavily.

Do I need separate content for AI search?

Usually not. The same helpful, well-structured content serves readers, traditional search and AI-generated answers. Focus on answering real questions clearly instead of writing for a particular system.

Does structured data help with AI answers?

Structured data helps machines understand what a page is about and can make it eligible for some rich results in search. It doesn’t guarantee that an AI system will use or cite the page.

Should I block AI crawlers?

It depends on your goals. Blocking training crawlers keeps your content out of some model training. Blocking crawlers used for AI search features may reduce your visibility in those features. Review each crawler’s documentation and decide case by case.
