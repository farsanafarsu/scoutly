SEO competitor analysis

SEO competitor analysis for keyword, content and backlink gaps

The sites you compete with in search results aren’t always your business competitors. Scoutly’s competitor analysis is designed to help you find out who actually competes for your topics, compare what they cover, and spot gaps where your site could give a more useful answer.

In development

Content and site-structure comparisons will use Scoutly’s own crawler. Keyword and backlink comparisons need external data providers, which aren’t connected in this build.

Who you’re up against

Your search competitors may surprise you

A local accounting firm might compete in search with national publishers, software companies and government guidance pages, not just with other firms down the street. Start by looking at who appears for the topics that matter to you, then choose a small set of competitors to compare.

  • Business competitors sell similar products or services.
  • Content competitors are publishers, blogs and communities that rank for your topics.
  • Answer competitors are the sources cited in featured snippets and AI-generated answers for your questions.

What to compare

Six ways to learn from the search landscape

Keywords they target

Which keywords competitors appear for that you don’t, based on available keyword data from a connected provider.

Topics they cover

Subjects and questions their content covers that are missing from yours, and topics they’ve missed too.

Pages with search visibility

The pages that seem to attract the most relevant visibility, and what makes them useful: format, depth and structure.

Referring domains

Sites that link to competitors, which may be relevant prospects for you. See the backlink checker.

Site structure

How competitors organize sections and internal links, and how that compares with your own navigation.

Answer quality

Where their answers are thin, outdated or hard to follow, and where your site could offer a more useful answer.

Content gap example

Spot topics competitors cover that you don’t

A content gap is a topic that competitors cover and you don’t. Seeing gaps side by side makes it easier to decide what to write next.

A gap isn’t automatically worth filling. Ask whether the topic fits your audience, and whether you can add something new, such as first-hand experience, better examples or a clearer structure, instead of producing another version of the same page.

From research to plan

Build around opportunities, not imitation

Build your strategy around relevant opportunities rather than copying what everyone else is doing. A practical way to work through competitor research:

  • Shortlist three to five competitors that appear for your most important topics.
  • Compare topic coverage, strong pages and referring domains.
  • Prioritize gaps by relevance to your audience and the effort to do them well.
  • Revisit the comparison periodically, because search results change.

FAQ

Competitor analysis questions

How many competitors should I analyze?

Three to five is usually enough. More than that tends to produce long lists that are hard to act on. Choose sites that appear for the topics you care about most.

Can Scoutly see my competitors’ traffic?

No tool has direct access to another site’s analytics. Third-party traffic figures are modeled estimates and can be far off. Where Scoutly shows estimates from a provider, it will label them as estimates, not measured data.

Is it okay to crawl a competitor’s website?

Competitor comparisons only fetch publicly available pages. Scoutly’s crawler respects robots.txt and stops at 25 pages per crawl.

What is a keyword gap?

A keyword gap is a set of keywords that one or more competitors appear for in search results, but your site doesn’t. It’s a starting point for research, not a list of keywords you must target.
