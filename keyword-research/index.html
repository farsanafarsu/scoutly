Keyword research

Keyword research for ideas, search intent and content planning

Good keyword research starts with what your audience actually searches for, and why. Scoutly’s keyword research workflow is designed to help you collect keyword ideas, group them by search intent and topic, and decide which ones deserve a page, with every metric labeled by where it came from.

Search intent

Start with what the searcher wants

The same topic can be searched with very different goals. Matching the type of page to the intent matters more than matching exact words.

Informational

“how to fix a redirect chain”

The searcher wants to learn. Guides, explanations and step-by-step tutorials fit best.

Navigational

“scoutly login”

The searcher wants a specific site or page. Make sure that page is easy to find and clearly titled.

Commercial

“best broken link checker”

The searcher is comparing options. Comparisons, feature pages and honest reviews fit best.

Transactional

“seo audit tool pricing”

The searcher is ready to act. Pricing, sign-up and product pages should answer them directly.

Evaluating opportunities

Questions to ask before you target a keyword

  • Is it relevant? Would someone searching this be glad to land on your site?
  • Can you match the intent? Check what kind of pages appear in results today: guides, product pages, videos or tools.
  • Do you already have a page for it? A second page on the same topic can end up competing with the first.
  • Is there demand? Volume estimates help you compare, but treat them as ranges, not exact counts.
  • Can you add something? First-hand experience, better examples or a clearer structure.

Topic clusters

Organize keywords into topics, not endless lists

A long keyword list is hard to act on. Grouping related keywords into topics helps you plan one strong page for each intent instead of many thin pages competing with each other.

A topic usually has one main page covering the subject broadly, supported by more specific pages that answer narrower questions and link back to it. That structure helps visitors find what they need, and helps search engines understand how your content relates. It also connects naturally to answer engine optimization, where clear answers to specific questions matter.

Where the numbers come from

Keyword metrics are estimates. Scoutly labels them.

Search volume, keyword difficulty and cost per click are modeled estimates from third-party providers, and different providers often disagree. Scoutly is designed to show where each number came from, so you know what you’re looking at.

  • Your own queries: Google Search Console reports the queries that actually led to impressions and clicks on your site. It’s the most reliable source for keywords you already appear for. Connecting your account is a planned integration.
  • Keyword ideas and volumes: from an external provider, shown with the source and date.
  • Cost before you spend: for paid lookups, Scoutly is designed to show an estimated cost first, so you decide.

FAQ

Keyword research questions

Where does keyword search volume come from?

Search engines don’t publish complete search volumes. Keyword tools estimate them from sources such as advertising data and clickstream panels, so numbers differ between providers. Use them to compare keywords rather than as exact counts.

What is keyword difficulty?

A provider’s estimate of how hard it may be to rank for a keyword, often based on the links pointing to the pages that currently rank. Every provider calculates it differently, so use it as a rough, relative guide.

How many keywords should one page target?

Focus each page on one main topic and intent. A good page often appears for many related searches naturally. Creating separate pages for near-identical keywords usually spreads your effort thin.

Can I use my Search Console data?

Connecting Google Search Console is a planned integration. Once connected, Scoutly will be able to read the queries, clicks and impressions for your verified sites, which is the most accurate picture of how people already find you.
